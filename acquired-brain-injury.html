Physiotherapy for Acquired Brain Injury

An acquired brain injury can affect movement, balance, coordination, strength, endurance and everyday activities. Physiotherapy can help adults work towards safer movement, greater independence and meaningful participation.

Request Physiotherapy →
CONDITION

Practical support for everyday function.

Physiotherapy is tailored to the person's abilities, environment and goals. Your physiotherapist will assess current function and develop a program around what is important to you.

PROBLEMS YOU MAY FACE
  • Difficulty walking or moving around safely
  • Poor balance or coordination
  • Reduced strength and endurance
  • Difficulty with transfers
  • Falls or fear of falling
  • Changes in movement control
  • Pain, stiffness or fatigue
GOALS
  • Walk more safely and confidently
  • Improve balance and reduce falls risk
  • Increase strength for everyday activities
  • Improve transfers
  • Increase walking endurance
  • Use mobility equipment safely
  • Participate more in the community
PHYSIOTHERAPY INTERVENTIONS
  • Functional movement and gait retraining
  • Balance and coordination training
  • Strength and endurance exercises
  • Transfer practice
  • Falls prevention strategies
  • Mobility aid assessment and training
  • Individual home exercise program
YOUR GOALS COME FIRST

Care built around what matters to you.

Depending on your circumstances, physiotherapy may support NDIS goals relating to mobility, independence, participation, safety, exercise and functional capacity. Outcomes vary between individuals and goals are reviewed as needs change.
