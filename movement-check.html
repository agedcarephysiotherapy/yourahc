Stand on one leg

Use a clear space and have something stable nearby to hold if needed.

LEG STRENGTH

30-second sit-to-stand

Start seated. Stand fully and sit down as many times as you can in 30 seconds. Count each full stand.

Use a firm chair. Do not perform the test if it is unsafe for you.

THE RESEARCH

Where the reference values come from

Single-leg stance

Springer et al. studied 549 healthy adults aged 18–99. Eyes-open performance decreased with age, and the study found performance was age-specific rather than gender-dependent.

View the study →
30-second sit-to-stand

Barros-Poblete et al. studied 499 healthy adults aged 18–80 and reported age- and sex-specific reference values for the 30-second sit-to-stand test, including the median and 25th–75th percentile range.

View the research →

These are reference values from research populations, not a diagnosis or a pass/fail test. The 30-second sit-to-stand reference data used here cover adults aged 18–80. Test technique, footwear, chair height, health conditions and other factors can affect results. If you are concerned about balance, falls or mobility, speak with a physiotherapist or other health professional.
