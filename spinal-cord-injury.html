Physiotherapy for Spinal Cord Injury

A spinal cord injury can affect movement, sensation, strength and physical independence. Physiotherapy can help adults maximise physical capacity and safely manage everyday mobility.

Request Physiotherapy →
HOW PHYSIOTHERAPY CAN HELP

Practical support for everyday function.

Physiotherapy is tailored to the person's abilities, environment and goals. Your physiotherapist will assess current function and develop a program around what is important to you.

PHYSIOTHERAPY MAY INCLUDE
  • Strength and conditioning
  • Transfer training
  • Wheelchair mobility
  • Standing and mobility programs where appropriate
  • Upper limb conditioning
  • Balance and postural control
  • Flexibility and joint mobility
  • Pain management strategies
  • Falls and injury prevention
  • Exercise prescription
  • Mobility equipment
  • Functional movement
  • Community mobility
GOALS WE MAY WORK TOWARDS
  • Improving transfer independence
  • Maintaining upper and lower limb strength
  • Improving wheelchair mobility
  • Increasing physical endurance
  • Improving sitting or standing balance
  • Maintaining joint movement
  • Increasing independence with daily activities
  • Safely participating in exercise
  • Improving confidence with mobility
  • Supporting community participation
  • Preventing avoidable loss of physical capacity
YOUR GOALS COME FIRST

Care built around what matters to you.

Depending on your circumstances, physiotherapy may support NDIS goals relating to mobility, independence, participation, safety, exercise and functional capacity. Outcomes vary between individuals and goals are reviewed as needs change.
