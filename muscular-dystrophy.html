Physiotherapy for Muscular Dystrophy

Muscular dystrophy refers to a group of genetic conditions that cause progressive muscle weakness. Physiotherapy needs to be carefully tailored to the person's condition, strength, fatigue and stage of disease.

Request Physiotherapy →
CONDITION

Practical support for everyday function.

Physiotherapy is tailored to the person's abilities, environment and goals. Your physiotherapist will assess current function and develop a program around what is important to you.

PROBLEMS YOU MAY FACE
  • Progressive muscle weakness
  • Reduced walking or mobility
  • Difficulty with transfers
  • Fatigue and reduced endurance
  • Joint tightness or reduced flexibility
  • Falls or reduced safety
  • Difficulty maintaining physical activity
GOALS
  • Maintain mobility and physical capacity
  • Maintain joint flexibility
  • Make transfers safer
  • Manage fatigue and conserve energy
  • Reduce falls and injury risk
  • Use mobility equipment safely
  • Support independence and participation
PHYSIOTHERAPY INTERVENTIONS
  • Appropriate low-load exercise
  • Flexibility and movement programs
  • Functional mobility training
  • Transfer strategies
  • Fatigue and energy management
  • Equipment and wheelchair advice
  • Carer and support worker education
YOUR GOALS COME FIRST

Care built around what matters to you.

Depending on your circumstances, physiotherapy may support NDIS goals relating to mobility, independence, participation, safety, exercise and functional capacity. Outcomes vary between individuals and goals are reviewed as needs change.
