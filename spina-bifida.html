SPINA BIFIDA

Physiotherapy for Spina Bifida

Spina bifida can affect muscle strength, sensation, balance and mobility. Physiotherapy can help adults maintain physical function and manage mobility challenges throughout life.

Request Physiotherapy →
CONDITION

Practical support for everyday function.

Physiotherapy is tailored to the person's abilities, environment and goals. Your physiotherapist will assess current function and develop a program around what is important to you.

PROBLEMS YOU MAY FACE
  • Reduced lower limb strength
  • Walking difficulties
  • Balance problems
  • Difficulty with transfers
  • Joint stiffness or reduced flexibility
  • Pain or physical discomfort
  • Reduced endurance or mobility
GOALS
  • Improve walking safety where appropriate
  • Maintain or improve strength
  • Improve balance
  • Make transfers safer
  • Maintain joint movement
  • Improve endurance
  • Use mobility equipment effectively and support independence
PHYSIOTHERAPY INTERVENTIONS
  • Strength and conditioning
  • Gait and mobility training
  • Balance exercises
  • Transfer training
  • Joint mobility and flexibility programs
  • Pain and physical function strategies
  • Mobility equipment advice
YOUR GOALS COME FIRST

Care built around what matters to you.

Depending on your circumstances, physiotherapy may support NDIS goals relating to mobility, independence, participation, safety, exercise and functional capacity. Outcomes vary between individuals and goals are reviewed as needs change.
